\subsection{The bosonic operator $\mathcal O_{HD}$ in the correlated
$V_1$ scenario}
\label{sec:v1-chd}

The neutral vector singlet
$V_1\sim(\mathbf 1,\mathbf 1)_0$ can couple not only to fermionic
currents, but also to the gauge-invariant Higgs current:
\begin{equation}
    J_H^\mu
    =H^\dagger i\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\mu}H
    \equiv
    iH^\dagger D^\mu H-i(D^\mu H)^\dagger H.
    \label{eq:v1-higgs-current}
\end{equation}
The relevant ultraviolet interactions may be written as:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal L_{V_1}
    =-\frac14 V_{1,\mu\nu}V_1^{\mu\nu}
      +\frac12M_{V_1}^2V_{1,\mu}V_1^\mu
      +V_{1,\mu}
       \left(
          \lambda_l J_l^\mu
          -\lambda_e J_e^\mu
          -\lambda_hJ_H^\mu
       \right)\label{eq:V1Lag}
\end{equation}
Here, $J_l^\mu=\bar l\gamma^\mu l$ and
$J_e^\mu=\bar e_R\gamma^\mu e_R$.  The parameter $\lambda_h$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:V1Lag} is real.  In terms of the raw
coupling used in the tree-level matching dictionary, the Hermitian
interaction corresponds to:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal D_{HH^\dagger V_1D}=\frac{i\lambda_h}{2}.
    \label{eq:v1-dictionary-h-coupling}
\end{equation}
Consequently, a statement such as ``real Higgs coupling $\lambda_h=1$'' refers to
the physical parameter $\lambda_h$ after extracting this factor of $i$; it does
not mean that the raw dictionary coupling
$\mathcal D_{HH^\dagger V_1D}$ is real.

At energies well below $M_{V_1}$, the leading solution of the classical
equation of motion is
\begin{equation}
    V_1^\mu
    =-\frac{1}{M_{V_1}^2}
       \left(
          \lambda_l J_l^\mu
          -\lambda_e J_e^\mu
          -hJ_H^\mu
       \right)
       +\mathcal O(M_{V_1}^{-4}).
\end{equation}
Substitution into Eq.~\eqref{eq:v1-current-lagrangian} gives
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal L_{\mathrm{SMEFT}}^{(6)}
    =-\frac{1}{2M_{V_1}^2}
       \left(
          \lambda_l J_l^\mu
          -\lambda_e J_e^\mu
          -hJ_H^\mu
       \right)^2.
    \label{eq:v1-current-square}
\end{equation}
The terms containing one Higgs current and one lepton current generate
$\mathcal O_{Hl}^{(1)}$ and $\mathcal O_{He}$.  By contrast, the square
of the Higgs current generates purely bosonic operators,
\begin{equation}
    \left(J_H^\mu J_{H,\mu}\right)
    \doteq
    \mathcal O_{H\Box}+4\mathcal O_{HD},
    \label{eq:higgs-current-warsaw-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\doteq$ denotes equality up to a total derivative and
\begin{align}
    \mathcal O_{H\Box}
    &=(H^\dagger H)\Box(H^\dagger H),
    \\
    \mathcal O_{HD}
    &=\left(H^\dagger D_\mu H\right)^*
      \left(H^\dagger D^\mu H\right).
\end{align}
It follows that the correlated $V_1$ model necessarily produces
\begin{equation}
    C_{H\Box}=-\frac{h^2}{2M_{V_1}^2},
    \qquad
    C_{HD}=-\frac{2h^2}{M_{V_1}^2}.
    \label{eq:v1-bosonic-matching}
\end{equation}
Thus, retaining the mixed coefficients proportional to
$h\lambda_{l,e}$ while discarding the bosonic coefficients proportional
to $h^2$ is not the complete tree-level matching of the same ultraviolet
benchmark.

\subsubsection{How $C_{HD}$ enters muon decay}

The operator $\mathcal O_{HD}$ contains no leptons and therefore does not
generate a four-lepton muon-decay amplitude directly.  It also does not
alter the charged-current $W\ell\nu$ vertex at tree level.  Its effect is
instead an electroweak-input effect.  In unitary gauge,
\begin{equation}
    H=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}
      \begin{pmatrix}0\\ v+\varphi\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
$\mathcal O_{HD}$ changes the neutral gauge-boson mass term but leaves the
charged one unchanged.  To first order in the dimension-six coefficient,
\begin{align}
    m_W^2
    &=\frac{g_2^2v^2}{4},
    \\
    m_Z^2
    &=\frac{(g_1^2+g_2^2)v^2}{4}
      \left(1+\frac12v^2C_{HD}\right).
    \label{eq:chd-gauge-boson-masses}
\end{align}
This unequal modification of the $W$ and $Z$ mass relations is the
custodial-symmetry-breaking effect associated with $\mathcal O_{HD}$.

Consider the electroweak input set
$\{\widehat m_W,\widehat m_Z,\widehat\alpha\}$.  Defining
\begin{equation}
    s_W\equiv\frac{g_1}{\sqrt{g_1^2+g_2^2}},
    \qquad
    c_W\equiv\frac{g_2}{\sqrt{g_1^2+g_2^2}},
\end{equation}
and holding the three measured inputs fixed, Eq.~\eqref{eq:chd-gauge-boson-masses}
implies
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\delta v}{v}
    =-\frac{c_W^2}{4s_W^2}\,v^2C_{HD}.
    \label{eq:chd-vev-shift}
\end{equation}
Since the Standard-Model charged-current normalization is
\begin{equation}
    G_F^{\mathrm{pred}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2v^2},
\end{equation}
the corresponding shift in the predicted Fermi constant is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\delta G_F^{\mathrm{pred}}}{G_F^{\mathrm{pred}}}
    =-2\frac{\delta v}{v}
    =\frac{c_W^2}{2s_W^2}\,v^2C_{HD}.
    \label{eq:chd-gf-shift}
\end{equation}
For the correlated $V_1$ matching in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:v1-bosonic-matching}, and with
\begin{equation}
    s_V\equiv\frac{v}{M_{V_1}},
\end{equation}
this becomes
\begin{equation}
    \left.
    \frac{\delta G_F^{\mathrm{pred}}}{G_F^{\mathrm{pred}}}
    \right|_{C_{HD}}
    =-\frac{c_W^2}{s_W^2}\,h^2s_V^2.
    \label{eq:v1-chd-gf-shift}
\end{equation}
The total non-radiative muon-decay rate is proportional to $G_F^2$.
Therefore, if $R_G$ denotes the rate normalization relative to the
Standard Model, the isolated linear contribution of $C_{HD}$ is
\begin{equation}
    \left.\Delta R_G\right|_{C_{HD}}
    =-2\frac{c_W^2}{s_W^2}\,h^2s_V^2.
    \label{eq:v1-chd-rate-shift}
\end{equation}
In particular, if the previously obtained correlated-mediator result
without the purely bosonic operators is
\begin{equation}
    R_G^{\mathrm{old}}
    =1+s_V^2+\frac52s_V^4,
\end{equation}
then the complete dimension-six prediction at linear order is instead
\begin{equation}
    R_G
    =1+
      \left[
          1-2\frac{c_W^2}{s_W^2}h^2
      \right]s_V^2
      +\mathcal O(s_V^4).
    \label{eq:v1-rate-with-chd}
\end{equation}
The coefficient multiplying $h^2s_V^2$ is numerically sizeable,
$2c_W^2/s_W^2\simeq6\text{--}7$, so the omission of $C_{HD}$ is not a
small correction when $h$ is taken to be of order unity.

At tree level, $C_{HD}$ changes only the overall normalization of the
muon-decay amplitude.  It therefore affects the lifetime, but not the
normalized Michel-spectrum shape parameters such as $\rho$, $\delta$ or
$\xi$.  The additional same-flavour-neutrino channels in the correlated
$V_1$ benchmark arise instead from $\mathcal O_{Hl}^{(1)}$ and
$\mathcal O_{He}$ through off-shell $Z$ exchange; they should not be
attributed to $\mathcal O_{HD}$.

\paragraph{Input-scheme qualification.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:chd-gf-shift} is visible because the electroweak
parameters are inferred from
$\{\widehat m_W,\widehat m_Z,\widehat\alpha\}$ and the muon lifetime is
then treated as an observable.  In an input scheme that uses the measured
$G_F$ itself, for example
$\{\widehat G_F,\widehat m_Z,\widehat\alpha\}$, this normalization shift
is absorbed into the definition of the electroweak vacuum expectation
value.  It must then not be counted a second time as an independent
muon-decay constraint; the effect of $C_{HD}$ reappears in predictions for
other electroweak observables.

\paragraph{EFT-order qualification.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:v1-rate-with-chd} is the result consistently truncated
at order $M_{V_1}^{-2}$.  Extending it to order $s_V^4$ requires the
squares and cross terms of all dimension-six contributions.  A complete
model-independent SMEFT prediction at that order also requires the
interference of dimension-eight operators with the Standard Model.  Thus,
one should either quote the linear dimension-six result or compute the
higher-order terms consistently within the specified ultraviolet model.
