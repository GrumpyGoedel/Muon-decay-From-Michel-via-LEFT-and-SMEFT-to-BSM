The muon was not welcomed quite enthusiastically by particle physicists when it made its first appearance in cloud chamber experiments in the 1930s \cite{neddermeyer1937note}: \enquote{Who ordered that?} was Isidor Rabi's famous reaction to the strange new particle \cite{bartusiak1987who}, encapturing the disorientation of a scientific community who firmly believed matter to consist of electrons, neutrons and protons. The muon fitted nowhere in this scheme: Sharing all its properties with the electron except having 200 times its mass, the muon seemed to be basically a heavy copy of the electron. 

But soon the muon took its place at the very heart of particle research, acting as a messenger for a plethora of unkown physics: Being the first heavy sibling of the elctron to be discovered, it introduced the three-generational structure of the Standard Model, where each matter field exists in three different versions of increasing mass. The muon decaying into an electron with varying energy proved that another new type of particle had to be involved in the process which carried the rest of the energy but was otherwise neutral and lightweight -- the neutrino. And when Louis Michel first determined the spectrum shape of the emitted electron by a single parameter $\rho$, experimental physicists could go on to resolve the precise nature of the electroweak interaction to be of V-A nature. So arguably muon decay has served for many decades as almost a kind of Rosetta stone for particle physics. But -- has its translative potential truly been exhausted yet?\par 
The V-A structure of electroweak interactions was later explained as being mediated by a heavy gauge field and the full theory of electroweak processes involves four gauge fields, three of which obtain mass when the scalar doublet they interact with obtains its vaccum expecation value of $\approx 246\text{ GeV}$. Their exact properties were revealed only later by specific experiments but many features of the heavy new particles could already be deduced from the precise phenomenology of muon decay. In the same way this process could be viewed as setting constraints for yet unknown physics. In order to determine precise boundaries for possible extensions of the Standard Model we apply a rigorous approach to bridge the energy regimes: At the scale of the muon mass the Low Energy Effective Field Theory (LEFT) is the adequate description for processes such as muon decay. We perturb the standard set of Michel parameters $\rho, \eta, \delta$ and $\xi$ of muon decay by the presence of LEFT operators -- first by the two operators that account for the classic signature $\mu^- \to e^- \bar{\nu}_e \nu_\mu$ and then by LEFT operators that allow for all possible combinations of neutrino output: Two neutrinos of any generation and with any lepton number $+1$ or $-1$. From experimental values for the Michel parameters we deduce the supression range of the LEFT operators. Then we begin the search for UV origins of the LEFT operators. To this end we first evolve the LEFT operators up to the electroweak scale, match their SMEFT origins onto them and subsequently evolve the SMEFT operators up to the energy scale of new physics. Here, we use a dictionary for SMEFT operators to collect possible UV fields which would produce them and therefore leave their imprint on muon decay. In this way we apply yet another precise reading of the muonic Rosetta stone and see whether the particle no one ordered has yet another thing to tell us.\par 
The structure of the thesis is as follows: The first chapter revolves around the Michel parameters of muon decay, whereby we first briefly introduce the Standard Model and how it describes muon decay. Subsequently the parameters themselves are introduced and how they shed light on the interaction involved in muon decay. Then the LEFT is introduced and how the presence of LEFT operators perturbs the Michel parameters. Finally the Renormalization group equations are applied to evolve the LEFT operators up to the electroweak scale.\par 
In the second chapter we delve into the Standard Model Effective Field Theory (SMEFT). After the being introduced in the first section, its matching conditions onto the LEFT are used to translate the derived constraints from the first chapter to the energy regime above the electroweak scale. The full one-loop RGEs for SMEFT dimension six and seven are then used to further evolve the constraints up to the scale of new physics.\par
We begin the fourth chapter by a short review of the general approaches of top-down and bottom-up effective field theories. After the short outline of its theoretical mechanisms a full dictionary for SMEFT operators is used to collect the UV origins of the identified operators.\par 
In the last chapter we present our results, namely the constraints for the UV origins of muon decay that stem from the exerimental values of the Michel parameters. After reflecting on the posed question of how muon decay thus has still to tell us about new physics we then give a short outlook on how the findings could be further analyzed and refined by possible new directions of investigation.
